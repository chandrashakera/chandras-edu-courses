Computer Arithmetic — Solved Problems

⏱ 25 min read 📌 Supplementary — beyond the syllabus
In simple terms: each problem below follows the exact register-by-register steps of the hardware algorithms in this unit — work them on paper first, then check each row.

These problems use the algorithms from Multiplication Algorithms, Division Algorithms and Floating-Point Arithmetic. Registers: BR (multiplicand / divisor), AC or A (accumulator), QR or Q (multiplier / quotient), Qn+1 (Booth’s extra bit), SC (sequence counter).

1. Booth’s Multiplication Algorithm

Rule: QnQn+1 = 10 → AC ← AC − BR; 01 → AC ← AC + BR; 00 or 11 → no operation; then arithmetic shift right (ashr) AC, QR, Qn+1; SC ← SC − 1; stop at SC = 0. Product = AC QR.

2. Division — Restoring and Non-Restoring

For restoring division, see the 448 ÷ 17 trace on the Division Algorithms page.

Non-restoring rule: shift A Q left; if A ≥ 0 subtract B, else add B; quotient bit = 1 if the new A ≥ 0, else 0; repeat n times; if the final A < 0, add B once. A is n + 1 bits so its sign is visible.

Problem 3 — 448 ÷ 17 by non-restoring division

Dividend 0111000000 (A = 001110, Q = 00000), B = 17 = 010001, −B = 101111.

StepOperationAQ
—Initial00111000000
1shl, A ≥ 0 → −B001011 (+11)00001
2shl, A ≥ 0 → −B000101 (+5)00011
3shl, A ≥ 0 → −B111001 (−7)00110
4shl, A < 0 → +B000011 (+3)01101
5shl, A ≥ 0 → −B110101 (−11)11010
—A < 0 → correct: +B000110 (+6)11010

Quotient 11010 = 26, remainder 6 ✓ (17 × 26 + 6 = 448).

Problem 4 — 449 ÷ 17 (non-restoring)

Dividend 0111000001 (A = 001110, Q = 00001).

StepOperationAQ
—Initial00111000001
1shl, −B001011 (+11)00011
2shl, −B000101 (+5)00111
3shl, −B111001 (−7)01110
4shl, +B000011 (+3)11101
5shl, −B110110 (−10)11010
—correct: +B000111 (+7)11010

Quotient 26, remainder 7 ✓ (17 × 26 + 7 = 449). The dividend’s low bits shift out of Q as quotient bits shift in.

Divide overflow: before starting, if the upper half of the dividend ≥ the divisor, the quotient will not fit — the hardware sets a divide-overflow flag.

3. Floating-Point Arithmetic

Problem 5 — (1.01 × 2³) + (1.10 × 2²)

This uses the IEEE-style normalised form 1.f × 2e (as in the question papers); the other problems use the textbook’s fraction form 0.1f × 2e — the steps are the same, only where the leading 1 sits differs.

Align (smaller exponent up):  1.10 × 2² = 0.110 × 2³
Add mantissas:                1.010 + 0.110 = 10.000  → × 2³
Normalise (shift right 1):    1.000 × 2⁴
Check: 10 + 6 = 16 = 1.000 × 2⁴ ✓

Problem 6 — (0.1110 × 2⁴) − (0.1010 × 2²)

Align:      0.1010 × 2² = 0.001010 × 2⁴
Subtract:   0.111000 − 0.001010 = 0.101110 × 2⁴   (already normalised — first bit after the point is 1)
Check: 14 − 2.5 = 11.5 = 0.101110 × 2⁴ ✓

Problem 7 — (0.1100 × 2³) × (0.1010 × 2²)

Add exponents:        3 + 2 = 5
Multiply mantissas:   0.1100 × 0.1010 = 0.01111000
Normalise (shift left 1, exponent − 1):  0.1111 × 2⁴
Check: 6 × 2.5 = 15 = 0.1111 × 2⁴ ✓

Problem 8 — (0.1100 × 2⁵) ÷ (0.1000 × 2²)

Dividend mantissa ≥ divisor mantissa → shift dividend right first:  0.0110 × 2⁶
Subtract exponents:   6 − 2 = 4
Divide mantissas:     0.0110 ÷ 0.1000 = 0.1100
Result: 0.1100 × 2⁴      Check: 24 ÷ 2 = 12 = 0.1100 × 2⁴ ✓

With biased exponents, remember: add exponents then subtract one bias (multiply); subtract exponents then add one bias (divide).

Common Pitfalls

  • Logical instead of arithmetic shift in Booth’s — ashr copies the sign bit into AC’s leftmost position.
  • Forgetting the final correction in non-restoring division when the last A is negative.
  • Aligning the wrong operand — always shift the mantissa of the number with the smaller exponent right.
