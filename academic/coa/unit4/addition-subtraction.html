Addition and Subtraction

⏱ 15 min read 📝 Quiz inside

The arithmetic unit of a computer must handle two common representations for negative numbers: signed-magnitude and signed-2's complement. Section 10-2 develops the hardware and algorithms for both.

Signed-Magnitude Addition and Subtraction

In signed-magnitude representation, numbers consist of a magnitude and a separate sign bit. When two numbers are added or subtracted, the actual operation to perform on the magnitudes depends on both the operation and the signs of the two operands. There are eight possible combinations of sign and operation, summarised in Table 10-1.

The rule reduces to two cases:

  • Add magnitudes when the signs are identical (for addition) or different (for subtraction), and attach the sign of A to the result.
  • Subtract magnitudes otherwise. If A ≥ B the result is positive with sign of A; if A < B the result is B − A with the complement of A's sign.

A special case arises when the subtraction produces a zero result — the sign must be forced to positive (+0, not −0).

Hardware Implementation — Fig. 10-1

Figure 10-1 shows the hardware for signed-magnitude addition and subtraction. The key observation is that subtraction of magnitudes is implemented using 2's complement addition, which eliminates the need for a separate subtractor circuit.

Fig. 10-1 — Hardware for signed-magnitude addition and subtraction

The components are:

  • B register — holds the magnitude of the second operand.
  • Complementer — controlled by mode bit M. When M = 0, B passes through unchanged; when M = 1, the 1's complement of B passes through and the input carry is set to 1, so the adder computes A + B̄ + 1 = A − B (2's complement subtraction).
  • Parallel adder — computes the sum. Its output carry goes to flip-flop E to indicate A ≥ B after subtraction.
  • A register — accumulates the result (sum S is loaded here).
  • Sign flip-flops Aₛ and Bₛ — hold the sign bits; their XOR determines whether to add or subtract magnitudes.
  • AVF (add-overflow flip-flop) — captures E (the carry) when magnitudes are added; signals overflow if the result exceeds the register capacity.

Hardware Algorithm — Fig. 10-2

Figure 10-2 is the flowchart for the hardware algorithm. The two columns represent the subtract and add operations separately; they share the same END node since both ultimately place the result in A and Aₛ.

Fig. 10-2 — Flowchart for add and subtract operations

Reading the left (subtract) column:

  1. Compute Aₛ ⊕ Bₛ. If the result is 0, the signs are the same — subtract magnitudes (EA ← A + B̄ + 1, AVF ← 0). If 1, add magnitudes (EA ← A + B, AVF ← E).
  2. On the subtract path, test E (the carry from the adder). E = 1 means A ≥ B — check if the result is zero (A = 0) and if so force Aₛ ← 0 to avoid −0. E = 0 means A < B — take the 2's complement of A (Ā, then +1) and complement the sign.

Signed-2's Complement Addition and Subtraction

Most computers use signed-2's complement representation. The algorithm is much simpler:

  • Addition: add the two numbers including their sign bits. Discard the carry out of the sign position. Check for overflow using V ← XOR(C₇, C₈).
  • Subtraction: take the 2's complement of the subtrahend and add it to the minuend.

The hardware (Fig. 10-3) is the same complementer-adder circuit but without separate sign flip-flops. The registers are named AC (accumulator) and BR (B register). Overflow flip-flop V is set when the XOR of the last two carries equals 1.

Overflow Detection

Overflow rule: overflow occurs in signed-2's complement addition when two numbers of the same sign are added and the result has the opposite sign. Hardware detects this as: V = C₇ ⊕ C₈, where C₇ is the carry into the sign bit and C₈ is the carry out of the sign bit.

Summary

  • Table 10-1 has 8 rows covering all sign/operation combinations for signed-magnitude arithmetic.
  • The complementer controlled by M implements both addition (M = 0) and subtraction (M = 1) with one adder circuit.
  • E = 1 after subtraction means A ≥ B; E = 0 means A < B — the result must be re-complemented.
  • Signed-2's complement arithmetic is simpler: one add for both operations; overflow detected by XOR of last two carries.
