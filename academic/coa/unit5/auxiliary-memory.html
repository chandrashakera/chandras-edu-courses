Auxiliary Memory

⏱ 15 min read 📝 Quiz inside

Auxiliary memory holds programs and data that are not currently needed by the CPU. It provides large-capacity, low-cost storage at the expense of slow access speed. The most common auxiliary memory devices are magnetic disks and magnetic tapes (§12-3).

General Characteristics

Any auxiliary storage device is characterised by five parameters: access mode, access time, transfer rate, capacity, and cost. The access time for a mechanical device has two components:

  • Seek time — time to position the read/write head over the correct track. This is the dominant component and is usually several milliseconds.
  • Transfer time — time to read or write data once the head is positioned.

Because seek time is much larger than transfer time, data is organised in records (blocks of characters or words). Reading or writing is always done in complete records to amortise the seek overhead.

Magnetic Disk — Fig 12-5

A magnetic disk is a circular plate of metal or plastic coated with magnetic recording material. Data is stored as magnetic spots along concentric circles called tracks. Each track is divided into sectors — the minimum addressable unit for a disk transfer.

A disk is addressed by four fields: disk number, disk surface, sector number, and track within the sector. After the head is mechanically moved to the correct track (seek), the system waits for the rotating disk to bring the correct sector under the head (rotational latency), then transfers the data.

Two types of disk units exist:

  • Hard disks — permanently attached, metal surfaces, high speed and capacity. Each disk surface may have one moveable head (cheaper) or one head per track (faster, more expensive).
  • Floppy disks — removable, flexible plastic coated with magnetic material, two standard sizes (5.25-inch and 3.5-inch). Used for software distribution and small data files.

Magnetic Tape

A magnetic tape is a strip of plastic coated with magnetic recording medium. Bits are recorded as magnetic spots across several parallel tracks (7 or 9 tracks simultaneously), forming one character per column. Read/write heads are mounted one per track.

Tape access is sequential — data can only be accessed in the order it is stored. Because a tape drive cannot stop and start instantaneously between individual characters, data is grouped into records separated by blank inter-record gaps. The tape stops in a gap and accelerates to full speed before reaching the next record. Each record has an identification bit pattern at its start and end.

Key difference — disk vs tape: Disk provides random access to any sector in any order; tape provides sequential access only. Disk is used for online active data; tape is used for archival backup and offline storage.

Summary

  • Auxiliary memory: large capacity, low cost, slow access; not directly addressable by the CPU.
  • Access time = seek time + rotational latency + transfer time; seek time dominates.
  • Disk: concentric tracks divided into sectors; addressed by disk/surface/sector/track; random access.
  • Hard disk: permanently attached. Floppy disk: removable, plastic substrate.
  • Tape: sequential access; data in records separated by inter-record gaps; used for backup and archival.
