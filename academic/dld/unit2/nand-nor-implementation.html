NAND/NOR Implementation

⏱ 18 min read 📝 Quiz inside 📖 Mano & Ciletti Ch. 3

Unit 1 showed that NAND and NOR are each individually universal — any Boolean function can be built from just one of them. This page makes that concrete: given a minimal SOP or POS expression straight from a K-map, there's a direct, mechanical way to redraw it using nothing but NAND gates (for SOP) or nothing but NOR gates (for POS) — no algebra required, just a wiring pattern.

SOP → NAND-NAND: the Two-Level Conversion

For a two-level SOP circuit (AND gates feeding a single OR gate), the conversion rule is simple: replace every gate — both the AND gates and the final OR gate — with a NAND gate, keeping all the same connections. The circuit computes the exact same function.

Why this works falls straight out of DeMorgan's law. For F = XY + ZW (two AND terms feeding an OR), replacing every gate with NAND gives:

((XY)′ · (ZW)′)′ = XY + ZW

The right-hand equality is exactly DeMorgan's law, (A′B′)′ = A+B, applied with A=XY and B=ZW. The double inversion at the second level exactly cancels out, leaving the original SOP function unchanged.

Worked Example — F = AB + BC

Two first-level NAND gates compute (AB)′ and (BC)′ (note B fans out to both — a shared input feeding two gates is completely normal); the final NAND combines them: ((AB)′·(BC)′)′ = AB+BC. Every gate in the circuit is a NAND — nothing else.

⚠️ This direct gate-substitution rule applies specifically to two-level SOP circuits. A literal that appears alone (not inside any AND term) needs a little extra care: a lone inverted literal already IS a NOT, which a tied-input NAND can produce directly — but a lone uncomplemented literal feeding straight into the final OR needs its own inverter pair (two cascaded tied-input NANDs) to correctly cancel out the extra inversion the conversion would otherwise introduce.

POS → NOR-NOR: the Mirror Conversion

The same idea, dualized: for a two-level POS circuit (OR gates feeding a single AND gate), replace every gate with a NOR gate. The justification is the dual DeMorgan's law, (A′+B′)′ = AB.

Worked Example — F = (A+B)(B+C)

Two first-level NOR gates compute (A+B)′ and (B+C)′; the final NOR combines them: ((A+B)′+(B+C)′)′ = (A+B)(B+C), by the same DeMorgan pattern as before, dualized. Again, every gate is a NOR — nothing else.

Common Pitfalls

  • Applying the direct substitution rule to circuits with more than two levels of logic. Multi-level circuits need a more careful, step-by-step bubble-pushing conversion — the simple "replace every gate" trick only guarantees correctness for exactly two levels.
  • Forgetting to handle lone (single-literal) terms specially — an uncomplemented literal feeding directly into a level-2 OR/AND needs an extra inverter stage when converting, or the direct substitution introduces an unwanted inversion.
  • Mixing up which conversion goes with which form. SOP converts to NAND-NAND; POS converts to NOR-NOR. Applying NOR-NOR to an SOP expression (or vice versa) does not produce an equivalent circuit.

Applications

  • Standard-cell chip fabrication overwhelmingly favors NAND and NOR gates over AND/OR at the transistor level (as Unit 1 covered) — this two-level conversion is exactly how a designer's SOP/POS minimization result becomes a real, fabricable NAND-only or NOR-only netlist.
  • Programmable Logic Devices (covered later in this course) are physically built as fixed AND-OR or NAND-NAND arrays — understanding this conversion is a direct prerequisite for understanding how a PLA or PAL is actually wired internally.
  • Logic synthesis tools in real chip design flows perform exactly this kind of gate-substitution and bubble-pushing automatically, as one of many optimization passes between a minimized Boolean expression and a manufacturable gate-level netlist.

This closes out Unit 2: Gate-Level Minimization — from the K-map grid itself through SOP, POS, don't-cares, and finally real NAND/NOR circuit realization.
