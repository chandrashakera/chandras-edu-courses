How Indian Companies Use GenAI

⏱ 12 min read 📝 Assignment inside

You now understand what GenAI is and which tools to use. This sub-module answers the question that matters most for your career: what is actually happening inside the companies you want to work at?

India's large IT services companies have moved beyond small experiments with GenAI. They are building platforms, deploying AI agents in client work and retraining their workforces. Instead of memorising a list of company announcements, which goes out of date in months, this sub-module teaches you the four patterns you will see across the industry, each with one real, dated example. Recognise the pattern, and you can talk sensibly about any company you interview with.

The scale of GenAI adoption in Indian IT

Before looking at the patterns, here is the macro picture from Nasscom's Strategic Review 2026 (released February 2026; figures are Nasscom estimates for FY26):

  • About $315 billion in total Indian tech industry revenue, around 6.1% growth over FY25.
  • $10–12 billion of that revenue estimated to come from AI work.
  • Headcount up about 2.3% to roughly 5.95 million (about 1.35 lakh net additions). Nasscom says the industry is still a net job creator.
  • Nasscom described FY26 as the year AI moved from experiments to function-specific deployment, with roles shifting towards deeper specialisation and higher AI fluency.

What this means for you: AI is now part of everyday delivery work, and hiring is growing more slowly than revenue. The freshers who stand out are the ones who can use AI well and check its output.

Pattern 1 — AI agents inside client operations

The first wave of GenAI was chatbots that answer questions. The current wave is AI agents: systems that plan steps and take actions inside business processes, such as checking a document, updating a record or routing a request, with humans supervising.

Example — TCS (as on May 2025): In its Annual Report 2024–25, TCS said it provides domain AI solutions for business operations featuring over 150 AI agents, and its chairman described a "human + AI" delivery model with AI agents working alongside employees.

In your interview: show you understand the difference between a chatbot (answers questions) and an AI agent (takes actions). Be ready to explain one task in your target domain that an agent could do, and where a human must still check its work.

Pattern 2 — Branded AI platforms for client delivery

Large IT companies package their AI tools, frameworks and reusable solutions into named platforms. These platforms are what they sell to clients and what their delivery teams use day to day.

Example — Infosys (launched 23 May 2023): Infosys launched Infosys Topaz, which it describes as an AI-first set of services, solutions and platforms using generative AI technologies.

In your interview: look up the AI platform name of the company you are interviewing with and read its official page. Knowing what it is, and what kind of problems it targets, shows you follow the company and not just general AI news.

Pattern 3 — AI across the software development lifecycle

Code generation is only one part of it. Companies are applying GenAI to requirements, design, coding, testing, documentation and modernising old systems, usually combining their own platforms with tools such as GitHub Copilot.

Example — HCLTech (launched 5 March 2024): HCLTech launched AI Force, a GenAI platform for the software development and engineering lifecycle. The company said it is built on Azure OpenAI and can be integrated with GitHub Copilot.

In your interview: describe how you actually use AI when you code, for example to explain an error, draft tests or write documentation, and how you check the result before trusting it. A clear "generate, then verify" habit is more convincing than claiming AI writes all your code.

Pattern 4 — Reskilling the whole workforce

Every large IT company is training employees at scale: basic AI literacy for everyone, deeper training for specialist roles, and rules on responsible use. This is why "AI fluency" now appears in fresher job descriptions.

Example — Wipro (announced 12 July 2023): When it launched Wipro ai360, Wipro said it would train all 250,000 employees on AI fundamentals and responsible use of AI over the following 12 months.

In your interview: frame your AI skills in terms of results and responsibility. "I used AI to cut the time for X, and I checked Y before submitting" is far stronger than "I know how to use ChatGPT."

Putting it together

  • Agents — AI is starting to take actions in business processes, not just answer questions.
  • Platforms — each company packages its AI work under its own platform names.
  • Software lifecycle — AI touches every stage of building software, not only writing code.
  • Reskilling — AI literacy and responsible use are now expected of every employee.

Company announcements change every quarter. Before any interview, read the company's latest annual report or official AI page, and check the date on whatever you quote.
