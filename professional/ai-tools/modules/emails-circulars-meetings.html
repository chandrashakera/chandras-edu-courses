Emails, Circulars and Meetings

⏱ 12 min read ✍️ 15 min practice 📝 Quiz inside

📥 Download: Office Message Templates (.docx) — a circular, a formal email, a meeting agenda and minutes, each with a ready prompt.

Words to know
  • Circular: an official notice sent to many people at once, such as all students or all departments.
  • Agenda: the list of topics for a meeting, shared before the meeting.
  • Minutes: the official record of a meeting: who attended, what was decided, and who will do what by when.
  • Tone: how a message sounds to the reader: polite, firm, friendly or formal.

What you will learn

  • How to write formal emails with AI: requests, reminders and replies.
  • How to write circulars that have every detail people need.
  • How to turn rough notes into an agenda, and into minutes.
  • How to fix the tone of a message without changing its facts.
  • What you must never paste into an AI tool.

1. The golden rule for office messages

AI writes the words. You give the facts, and you check them.

Office messages go to many people. One wrong date can send 300 students to an empty hall. So every request in this module ends with the same safety line:

Use only the information I give. Write [TO FILL] for anything missing. Do not make up names, dates, numbers or places.

You met this idea in Modules 2 and 4. Here you will use it every time.

2. Formal emails

Most office emails are one of three types:

TypeExampleWhat the AI needs from you
RequestAsking the Principal's permission to use the seminar hallWhat you need, when, and why
ReminderReminding departments to send attendance reportsWhat is pending, and the new deadline
ReplyAnswering a parent's question about fee receiptsThe message you received, and what you want to say

Prompt: write a formal email

Formal email
You are an office assistant at an engineering college. Write a formal email. To: [who, e.g. all Heads of Departments] From: [your role, e.g. Office Assistant, Examination Branch] Purpose: [request / reminder / reply] Facts: [all the details: what, when, where] What I want them to do: [action] by [date] Use the greeting [e.g. Dear Sir/Madam] and the closing [e.g. Regards]. Write a clear subject line. Polite and formal. Under 120 words. Use only the information I give. Write [TO FILL] for anything missing. Do not make up names, dates or numbers.

Tip: tell the AI the greeting and closing your office uses. Every office has its own habit, and the AI cannot guess it.

Prompt: reply to an email you received

Reply to an email
Here is an email I received. I have removed names and contact details. [paste the email] Write a polite reply that [what you want to say, e.g. apologises for the delay and says the receipts will be ready on [date]]. Do not promise anything I have not said. Formal tone. Under 120 words. Write [TO FILL] for anything missing.

"Do not promise anything I have not said" is important in replies. AI likes to be helpful, so it may add lines like "we will refund your fee" that your office never agreed to.

3. Circulars and notices

A good circular answers every question before people ask it. Use this checklist:

PartExample
Reference number and dateRef. No. and date, as your office writes them
SubjectAnnual Sports Day
Who it is forAll students and staff
What, when, whereEvent, date and day, time, venue
What to do, and by whenRegister with the Physical Director by 5 December
ContactWho to ask for details
SignatureWho signs, e.g. Principal

Prompt: write a circular

Circular
You are an office assistant at an engineering college. Write a circular. For: [who, e.g. all students and staff] Subject: [event or matter] Details: [what, date and day, time, venue] What readers must do: [action] by [date] Contact: [person or office] Signed by: [who signs] Formal tone. Under 150 words. Reference number and date lines as [TO FILL]. Main details as a short list. Use only the information I give. Write [TO FILL] for anything missing. Do not make up names, dates, numbers or places. Do not add the day of the week unless I give it.

Check it: the 5W check

Before you send any circular, check these five things against your own notes, not against the AI's text:

  1. What: is the event or matter correct?
  2. When: are the date, day and time correct? Check the day on a calendar.
  3. Where: is the venue correct?
  4. Who: is it addressed to the right people, and signed by the right person?
  5. What to do: is the action and deadline clear?
Watch the day of the week. AI tools often get the day wrong. For example, a draft may say "Friday, 14 November 2026", but 14 November 2026 is a Saturday. Always check the day on a calendar.

Short notice for a notice board or WhatsApp group

After the circular is ready, you can ask:

Short notice
Make a short version of this circular for a notice board. Under 50 words. Keep every date, time and place exactly the same.

4. Meetings: the agenda before, the minutes after

The agenda

Meeting agenda
Write an agenda for a meeting. Meeting: [e.g. Department staff meeting] Date, time and venue: [details] Chairperson: [role, e.g. Head of the Department] Topics: [list the topics in order] Start with "Confirmation of the minutes of the previous meeting". End with "Any other matter with the permission of the Chair". Number the items. Use only the topics I give.

The minutes, from your rough notes

During the meeting, write rough notes: short lines, in any order. After the meeting, AI can turn them into neat minutes.

Minutes from rough notes
Turn my rough notes into formal minutes of a meeting. Meeting: [name, date, time, venue] Chairperson: [role] Present: [list of roles or names, or write "see attendance register"] My notes: [paste your rough notes] Format: one numbered item for each topic, with a short summary and the decision. Then a table with three columns: Action, By whom, By when. Do not add any decision or action that is not in my notes. If a point in my notes is unclear, mark it [CHECK].

Two rules for minutes:

  1. AI must not invent decisions. Minutes are an official record. The line "Do not add any decision that is not in my notes" is the most important part of this prompt.
  2. Draft first, then approval. Send the draft to the chairperson to check. Circulate it only after they approve it.
Recording meetings: some apps can record a meeting and write notes. Before you record, follow your college's rules and tell everyone in the meeting. Never paste confidential discussions into an AI tool.

5. Fixing the tone

Sometimes the facts are right, but the message sounds wrong.

ProblemAsk the AI to make it...
Too harsh: "You have AGAIN not submitted the report."polite but firm
Too long: a full page for a simple remindershort, under 60 words
Too casual: "Hey all, pls send stuff by Friday"formal

Prompt: fix the tone

Fix the tone
Rewrite this message to sound [polite but firm / shorter / formal]. Keep every fact, name, date and number exactly the same. Under [number] words. [paste the message]

After the AI rewrites it, check that every date and number is still the same. When AI shortens a message, it sometimes drops a detail, such as the deadline.

6. What you must never paste

Office work has a lot of personal data. Before you paste anything into an AI tool, remove:

  • Phone numbers, personal email addresses and home addresses
  • Aadhaar, PAN or any ID numbers
  • Marks or attendance with student names
  • Salary details, and medical or personal reasons for leave
  • Anything marked confidential

Write a placeholder instead, such as [Student A] or [phone]. When the AI's draft is ready, copy it into Word and type the real details yourself. (Module 13 covers privacy in more detail.)

What about the AI button inside my email app?

Some email apps have a built-in AI writing button. For example, Gmail's "Help me write" is free for personal accounts in the United States, but in many other countries, including India, it needs a paid Google plan or an eligible work account. (Last checked: November 2026.)

The prompts in this module work in any free chat AI tool. Write the draft there, check it, then copy it into your email.

See the difference

Situation: An office assistant at Tarangini Institute of Technology (a made-up college) needs a circular for the Annual Sports Day.

Before: They type: "write circular for sports day". The AI writes a cheerful circular with a made-up date, says the event is at "the college ground" (the college calls it the playground), and promises "exciting prizes worth ₹50,000!". Nobody told the AI any of this.
After

They use the circular prompt with the real facts:

For: all students and staff. Subject: Annual Sports Day. Details: Saturday, 12 December 2026, 9:00 am to 4:00 pm, college playground; events: athletics, kabaddi, volleyball, chess. What readers must do: register with the Physical Director by Saturday, 5 December 2026. Signed by: Principal.

The AI writes:

TARANGINI INSTITUTE OF TECHNOLOGY
Ref. No.: [TO FILL]   Date: [TO FILL]
CIRCULAR
Subject: Annual Sports Day
All students and staff are informed that the Annual Sports Day will be held as follows:
  • Date: Saturday, 12 December 2026
  • Time: 9:00 am to 4:00 pm
  • Venue: College playground
  • Events: Athletics, kabaddi, volleyball and chess
Students who wish to take part must register with the Physical Director by Saturday, 5 December 2026.
For details, contact: [TO FILL]
Principal

Every fact comes from the request. The one missing detail (the contact) shows up as [TO FILL], so the assistant notices it and fills it in before sending.

Try it (15 minutes)

  1. Download the Office Message Templates.
  2. Pick one real message you need to send this week: an email or a circular. Remove personal details. Use the matching prompt.
  3. Do the 5W check on the AI's draft. Fix anything that is wrong.
  4. Minutes: take rough notes from a recent meeting. Remove anything confidential. Turn them into minutes with the prompt. Did the AI add anything that was not decided?
  5. Save your best prompt in your Prompt Library Workbook (Module 4). It can be one of the 5 prompts for your Final Project.

Remember these points

  • AI writes the words; you give the facts and check them.
  • End every request with the safety line: use only my information, write [TO FILL] for anything missing, do not make anything up.
  • Use the 5W check before sending a circular, and check the day of the week on a calendar.
  • For minutes, tell the AI not to add any decision that is not in your notes. Get the chairperson's approval before circulating.
  • When fixing tone, check that every fact and date stayed the same.
  • Never paste personal data. Use placeholders like [Student A], and type the real details yourself afterwards.
