Save Time: Your Own Prompt Collection

⏱ 10 min read ✍️ 20 min practice 📝 Quiz inside

📥 Download: Prompt Library Workbook (.docx) — you will also use it for your Final Project.

Words to know
  • Prompt collection (prompt library): your own document of requests that worked well, ready to copy and reuse.
  • About-me settings: a short note about you that the AI reads before every chat.
  • Project: a folder inside an AI tool that keeps your instructions and files for one type of work.

What you will learn

  • Why typing the same details again and again wastes time.
  • How to build your own prompt collection.
  • How to write about-me settings.
  • How to use a project folder for work you do often.
  • What the Final Project asks you to do.

1. The problem: typing the same thing again and again

In Module 2 you learned that a good request needs details. But if you write circulars every week, typing "I am an office assistant in an engineering college, we write formal circulars with a reference number..." every time is tiring.

There are 3 ways to write the details once and reuse them:

WayWhat it isWorks inHow long it lasts
1. Prompt collectionYour own Word file of requests that worked wellAny AI toolForever. It is your file.
2. About-me settingsA short note the AI reads before every chatOnly the tool where you set itUntil you change it
3. Project folderInstructions and files for one type of workOnly the tool where you set itUntil the tool changes the feature

Start with Way 1. It is the most important, and it works with every tool.

2. Way 1: Your prompt collection

A prompt collection is a document where you save requests that worked well for you. The parts that change each time go in [square brackets].

Why it helps

  • No blank page. Copy, fill in the brackets, paste.
  • Works in every AI tool. If a tool changes, or you switch tools, your collection still works.
  • Gets better over time. When you improve a request, update it in the collection.
  • Can be shared. A department or lab team can share one collection of requests that work.
Why your own file matters: AI tools change their features often. In late 2026, for example, OpenAI and Google both announced big changes to their "custom assistant" features. People who saved their instructions only inside those features had to move them. Your own file is never affected.

What to save for each prompt

ItemExample

How to arrange it

Group your prompts by type of work:

  • Messages: circulars, emails, notices, minutes
  • Teaching: lesson plans, question papers, feedback
  • Lab: lab manual pages, safety rules, stock registers, simple scripts
  • Data: Excel formulas, cleaning data, charts
  • Documents: reports, accreditation drafts
  • Personal: summarising, planning, learning something new

Start with 5 prompts. Add one whenever a request works well.

3. Way 2: About-me settings

Most chat tools let you write a short note about yourself. The AI reads it before every chat. Look in Settings for a name like Personalisation, Custom instructions, Profile or Saved info.

What to write

Use this template:

About-me template
About me: I work as [your job, e.g. lab assistant] in [type of college, e.g. an engineering college in India]. My usual work: [e.g. lab manuals, stock registers, safety notices]. Who reads my work: [e.g. students, faculty, the Principal]. My style: [e.g. simple English, short sentences]. Always: [e.g. write [TO FILL] for missing details; write dates as DD-MM-YYYY]. Never: [e.g. make up names, dates or numbers].

What NOT to write

The AI tool stores this note. Keep it general:

  • ✅ "I am a lab assistant in an engineering college."
  • ❌ Your phone number, employee ID, students' names or anything confidential.

Keep a copy of your about-me note in your prompt collection. If you change tools, paste it into the new one.

4. Way 3: Project folders

A project is a folder inside an AI tool. (It is called a Project in ChatGPT and Claude, and a notebook in NotebookLM. Features differ by tool.) It keeps:

  • Instructions for one type of work, and
  • Files the AI should use every time.

Every chat you start inside the project uses both automatically.

Example: a "Circulars" project

  • Instructions: "You help write official circulars. Follow the attached format exactly. Formal tone. Write [TO FILL] for missing details. Never make up names, dates or numbers."
  • Files: a blank circular format and one sample circular with names removed.

Now a new circular needs just one line:

Circular: blood donation camp, 5 December, 10 am–3 pm, college auditorium, organised by NSS unit, all students and staff welcome.

Project ideas for your job

Your roleProject ideas
Teaching staff"Lesson plans for [subject]" with the syllabus and your lesson plan format
Office staff"Circulars and notices" with your formats; "Minutes" with your minutes format
Lab assistants and system programmers"Lab documents" with your lab manual page format; "Equipment" with the equipment manuals
Remember: upload only files you are allowed to share, and never files with students' or staff members' personal data. Free plans usually limit how many files a project can hold, so upload formats and samples, not whole folders.

Saved assistants

Some tools also let you save a named assistant with its own instructions. For example, Google's Gems are being changed into a feature called "skills." These are useful, but treat them like projects: keep the main copy of your instructions in your prompt collection.

See the difference

Situation: Every Monday, an office assistant at Tarangini Institute of Technology (a made-up college) writes 3 or 4 circulars.

Before: They type the full request from memory every time. One week they forget to write "do not make up details." The AI adds a made-up venue, and it nearly goes out to all students.
After
  1. The circular request is saved in their prompt collection.
  2. Their about-me settings say they work in a college office and use formal English.
  3. A "Circulars" project holds the blank format, a sample, and the instruction "write [TO FILL] for missing details; never make anything up."

Each circular now starts with one line. The safety instructions are always there, so missing details show up as [TO FILL] instead of made-up ones.

Try it (20 minutes)

  1. Download the Prompt Library Workbook.
  2. Write your about-me note using the template in Section 3. Save it in the workbook. Then paste it into your main chat tool's settings.
  3. Add 3 prompts to your collection, for jobs you do often. Use your best prompt from Module 2 as the first one.
  4. Extra: Create one project with instructions and a blank format from your own work. Test it with a one-line request.

Your Final Project (preview)

The last step before the certificate is a small project: My AI Work Kit. You will:

  • Pick one real job you do often (for example, a weekly circular, a stock register, or a lesson plan).
  • Bring 5 prompts from your prompt collection that you have tried yourself. One of them is for your chosen job.
  • Use that prompt to make one finished document you can really use.
  • Answer 3 short questions about what you checked and how much time you saved.

It takes about one hour. At the end, you can print or save your kit as a PDF and keep using it.

Keep adding to the workbook as you go through the course. By the end, most of your project will already be ready.

Remember these points

  • Typing the same details again and again wastes time. Write them once and reuse them.
  • Your prompt collection is the most important thing you build. It works in every tool and never disappears.
  • About-me settings tell the AI your job and style. Keep them general, with no personal data.
  • Project folders keep instructions and files for one type of work.
  • Tools change, so keep the main copy of your instructions in your prompt collection.
