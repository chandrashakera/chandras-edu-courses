Picking the Right AI Tool

⏱ 10 min read ✍️ 15 min practice 📝 Quiz inside
Last checked: September 2026. AI tools change their features and free limits often. This is the only module that talks about specific tools in detail. We update it regularly, so the rest of the course stays correct.
Words to know
  • Free plan: the version of an AI tool you can use without paying. It has daily or hourly limits.
  • Upload: to give the AI a file (like a PDF) from your computer or phone.
  • Source: the website or document an answer comes from.

What you will learn

  • The 5 types of AI tools and what each is best at.
  • 4 quick questions to pick the right tool.
  • How to manage with free plans only.
  • Which settings to check before you start.

1. Learn the type of tool, not the buttons

New AI tools come out every month, and old ones keep changing. If you only learn "which button to click," that can be out of date in a few weeks. If you learn which type of tool suits which job, that stays useful.

There are 5 types of AI tools:

Type of toolBest forExamples (all have free plans)
1. Chat toolWriting, rewriting, shortening, explaining, ideas, Excel formulasChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot
2. Search toolFinding up-to-date information on the internet, with linksPerplexity; also the "search" option inside chat tools
3. My-files toolAnswering questions only from files you upload, like a manual, rulebook or syllabus, and showing where in the file the answer isNotebookLM; also "Projects" in ChatGPT and Claude
4. Slides and design toolMaking slides or posters from your textGamma, Canva's AI features
5. AI inside Office appsWorking directly inside Word, Excel, Gmail or Google DocsCopilot in Microsoft 365, Gemini in Google Workspace (usually depends on your college's account)

Many chat tools can now also search the internet and read files. But each type is still built mainly for one job, and usually does that job best.

2. Four questions to pick the right tool

Before you start, ask yourself:

  1. Is it mainly writing? → Use a chat tool.
  2. Do I need up-to-date facts from the internet? → Use a search tool (or a chat tool with search turned on). Then open the links and check.
  3. Must the answer come from a file I have? → Use a my-files tool. Upload the file and ask.
  4. Do I need slides or a poster? → Write the text in a chat tool first, then use a slides and design tool.

Why the my-files tool is so useful

Many questions at work are answered by documents: equipment manuals, college rules, lab manuals, syllabi and official circulars.

  • A chat tool answers from general knowledge. For your college or your equipment, that may be wrong.
  • A my-files tool answers from your file, and usually shows the exact part it used. You can click and check.
It can still misread a file. For anything important, always click and read the part it quoted.

3. Managing with free plans

Every tool above has a free plan. Free plans usually give you:

  • Normal chatting, with a limit that resets after a few hours or the next day.
  • Internet search and file upload, with lower limits than paid plans.
  • Less access to the newest features.

Tips:

  • Keep two chat tools ready. If you reach the limit in one, continue in the other.
  • Write one clear request instead of many small ones. A good 4-part request (Module 2) needs fewer follow-ups, so you use less of your limit.
  • Start a new chat for each new job. Very long chats use up your limit faster.
  • You do not need to pay. Every task in this course works on free plans.

4. Settings to check before you start

4.1 Which account are you using?

  • Personal account (your own Gmail or email): you control the settings.
  • College account (your college's Google or Microsoft login): your college may have turned some AI features on or off, with different data rules. Ask your IT or systems administrator which AI tools are allowed for official work.

4.2 Data settings

Most AI tools have a setting that controls whether your chats can be used to improve the AI. The name and place of this setting differ between tools. Look in Settings, under names like Data controls, Privacy or Activity. Read it once and choose on purpose.

Even with this setting turned off, follow the Traffic Light Rule from Module 1: never paste personal or confidential data into an AI tool.

4.3 Memory

Some AI tools remember things about you from past chats. This can be helpful (Module 4 shows how). But look at what it has remembered from time to time, and delete anything you do not want it to keep.

See the difference

Situation: A lab assistant at Tarangini Institute of Technology (a made-up college) needs the correct steps to calibrate a digital storage oscilloscope in the lab.

Wrong tool

In a chat tool, with no file: How do I calibrate a digital oscilloscope?

The answer is general. It may not match this model at all. Some steps may even be wrong for this equipment.

Right tool
  1. Open a my-files tool (for example, NotebookLM).
  2. Upload the oscilloscope's user manual PDF (from the manufacturer's website or the CD that came with it).
  3. Ask: "According to this manual, what are the steps to calibrate this oscilloscope? Show which page each step is from."
  4. Click each reference to check that the steps really come from the manual.

Now the answer comes from the actual manual, and you can see exactly where.

Try it (15 minutes)

Test 1: One request, two tools. Give the same 4-part request from Module 2 to two different chat tools. Which answer needed less editing? Your favourite tool may be different for different jobs.

Test 2: My-files tool.

  1. Take a file you are allowed to share, with no personal data: an equipment manual, a syllabus, a public rulebook or a published circular.
  2. Upload it to NotebookLM, or to a Project in ChatGPT or Claude.
  3. Ask three questions whose answers you already know. Check each answer against the file.

Test 3: Check your settings. In the AI tool you use most, open Settings. Find the data setting and the memory setting. Choose each one on purpose.

Remember these points

  • Learn the type of tool, not the buttons: chat, search, my-files, slides and design, and AI inside Office apps.
  • Ask the 4 questions: writing? internet facts? my file? slides?
  • For questions about your college or your equipment, use a my-files tool with the real document.
  • Free plans are enough. Keep two tools ready and write clear requests.
  • Check your account, data settings and memory before using AI for work.
