How AI Tools Work, and When They Go Wrong

⏱ 10 min read ✍️ 10 min practice 📝 Quiz inside
Words to know
  • AI tool: a website or app where you type a request and it writes a reply. Examples: ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot.
  • Prompt: the request you type to the AI.
  • Hallucination: when the AI gives wrong information that sounds correct.

What you will learn

  • How an AI tool writes its answers.
  • What AI does well, and where it makes mistakes.
  • A simple rule to decide what to use AI for: the Traffic Light Rule.

1. How does an AI tool write its answers?

An AI tool has read a huge amount of text: books, websites and articles. From this, it learned how words and sentences usually go together.

When you ask a question, the AI does not look up the answer in a book. It writes a new answer, word by word. It picks the words that fit best.

This explains two things:

  • ✅ It can write smoothly on almost any topic.
  • ⚠️ Sometimes it writes something that sounds right but is wrong.
Remember: AI writes answers that sound correct. They are usually correct, but not always. You must check anything important.

Some AI tools can also search the internet or read a file you give them. This makes answers more reliable, because they are based on a real source. But the AI can still misread the source, so check it.

2. What is AI good at?

AI is best at working with words you give it and at writing a first draft.

AI is good atExample from college work
Writing a first draftA circular for a guest lecture
Making writing clearer or more politeRewriting a stiff email to a parent
Making things shorterA half-page summary of a long report
Changing the layoutTurning rough meeting notes into proper minutes
ExplainingExplaining how an oscilloscope works, in simple words
Giving ideasTen ideas for a lab safety poster
Excel helpWriting a formula to calculate attendance percentage

In every case, you check and finish the work. The AI saves you from starting with a blank page.

3. Where does AI go wrong?

3.1 It can make things up

The AI may give a wrong fact, date, rule, number or reference, and say it very confidently. This is called a hallucination. The AI is not lying. It is writing words that look like a good answer.

Common examples:

  • A last date for a scholarship that is not the real date.
  • A book or research paper that does not exist.
  • A technical specification (like a voltage rating) that is slightly wrong.

How to check:

  • For dates and rules: open the official website or notice.
  • For equipment details: check the manufacturer's manual.
  • For books and papers: search the exact title in Google Scholar. If you cannot find it, do not use it.

3.2 It does not know your college

The AI does not know your college's rules, timetable, staff or lab equipment, unless you tell it. If you ask, it may guess instead of saying "I don't know."

3.3 It can get numbers wrong

AI is good at writing an Excel formula. But for actual calculations, especially on big tables, let Excel or Google Sheets do the maths. (Module 6 shows how.)

3.4 It gives a different answer each time

Ask the same question twice and you may get two different answers. This is normal. For important work, read carefully and edit.

4. Think of AI as a new helper

Imagine a new helper who joined your office yesterday:

  • Works very fast and writes well.
  • Knows a lot of general things.
  • Knows nothing about your college yet.
  • Sometimes says wrong things very confidently.
  • Never gets tired of redoing work.

You would not let a new helper send a notice to all students without reading it first. Treat AI the same way. You check. You are responsible.

5. The Traffic Light Rule

Use this rule before using AI for any job.

LightWhat it meansExamples
🟢 Green: go aheadLow risk. Just read it before you use it.Drafting an email, making text clearer, getting ideas, formatting notes
🟡 Yellow: check firstUseful, but check facts and numbers before you use them.Dates, rules, technical specifications, formulas, summaries of long documents, anything going to many people
🔴 Red: stopDo not share this data with AI, or do not let AI decide.Student names, marks, phone numbers or Aadhaar numbers; confidential files like unreleased question papers; final decisions about marks or discipline
Easy test: If you would not put it on a public notice board, do not paste it into an AI tool. Remove names and personal details first, or use made-up data.

Module 13 explains safe use in more detail.

See the difference

Situation: An office staff member is asked when the last date is for a national scholarship application.

Risky way

Prompt: What is the last date for the [scholarship name] application this year?

The AI gives a date confidently. The staff member puts it in a notice. The date is wrong. Students miss the real deadline, or rush for no reason.

Safe way
  1. Use an AI tool that searches the internet and shows links (Module 3 explains which).
  2. Ask: "Find the official page for the [scholarship name] application this year. Give me the link."
  3. Open the official page and read the date yourself.

The AI helped you find the page quickly. The fact came from the official source.

Try it (10 minutes)

Open any free AI tool (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude or Copilot). Try these three small tests.

Test 1: Does it know your college?

What are the working hours of the office at [your college's name]?

Does it say "I don't know," or does it guess? Compare with the real answer.

Test 2: Does it make things up?

Give me the full technical specifications of [a piece of equipment you know well, e.g. a model of power supply or computer in your lab].

Compare with the real manual. What did it get right? What was wrong?

Test 3: Where does it shine?

Paste a short notice or email you wrote recently (remove all names first). Then type:

Rewrite this to be clearer and more polite. Keep it under 100 words.

Compare how useful this was with Tests 1 and 2.

Think: Write down one job from your own work for each light: one green, one yellow and one red.

Remember these points

  • AI writes answers that sound right. It does not always know the right answer.
  • AI is great at drafting, rewriting, shortening, reformatting and explaining.
  • AI can make things up and does not know your college.
  • Treat AI like a new helper: fast and useful, but you check everything.
  • Use the Traffic Light Rule: 🟢 go ahead, 🟡 check first, 🔴 stop.
