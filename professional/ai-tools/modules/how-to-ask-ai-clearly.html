How to Ask AI Clearly

⏱ 12 min read ✍️ 15 min practice 📝 Quiz inside

📥 Download: Prompt Builder Worksheet (.docx)

Words to know
  • Prompt: the request you type to the AI.
  • Template: a blank format, like your college's circular format, that you fill in.
  • [TO FILL]: a marker the AI writes where information is missing, so you can fill it in yourself.

What you will learn

  • Why short, unclear requests give poor answers.
  • The 4 parts of a clear request.
  • How to improve the AI's answer by asking for changes.
  • How to make the AI fill in your college's own format.
  • Simple phrases that stop the AI from making things up.

1. Why short requests give poor answers

Remember Module 1: the AI knows nothing about your situation unless you tell it. If your request is too short, the AI has to guess. Its guesses are general and often useless.

Short request: Write a circular about a workshop.

The AI does not know: Which workshop? For whom? When? Where? Who is sending it? So it writes a general circular with blanks, or worse, it invents a date and place.

The fix is simple: give the AI the same details you would give a new helper.

2. The 4 parts of a clear request

PartAsk yourselfExample
1. Who should the AI act as? (Role)Who would normally do this job?"You are an office assistant in an engineering college."
2. What details does it need? (Context)What would a new helper need to know?"The ECE department is holding a 2-day IoT workshop for III B.Tech students on 14–15 November, 9:30 am to 4:30 pm, in Seminar Hall 2. Free. Only 60 seats. Register at the ECE office by 10 November."
3. What job should it do? (Task)What exactly do I want?"Write a circular to announce this workshop to students."
4. How should the answer look? (Format)How long? What style? What layout?"Formal. Under 150 words. A subject line, the main details as a short list, and a signature line for the Head of Department."

You do not need to write the parts in this order. You just need all four.

The most important part: details

Most poor answers happen because details are missing. Quick test:

Could a new helper do this job well with only what I wrote? If not, add the missing details.

Useful details include:

  • Who will read it: students, parents, the Principal, lab users.
  • Why: to inform, remind, request or apologise.
  • Facts: dates, times, places, names of events, last dates.
  • Limits: word limit, tone, what to avoid.

3. Two habits that improve every answer

Habit 1: Show an example

If you want a certain style, paste an old example in that style:

Here is a circular our office sent last month. Write the new circular in the same style.
[paste the old circular, with names removed]

One good example often works better than a long explanation.

Habit 2: Ask for changes

The first answer is only a first draft. Reply with short instructions, like:

  • "Make it shorter, under 100 words."
  • "Make it more formal."
  • "Put the details in a table."
  • "The date is wrong. It is 15 November."
  • "Give me two more versions."

Asking for two or three changes is normal. It is usually faster than trying to write a perfect request the first time.

4. Make AI use your college's format

Every college has its own formats: for circulars, minutes, lab records and reports. The examples in this course use a made-up college. You should always use your own formats.

Here is how:

Below is our college's format for [type of document]. Fill it in using the details I give.
Keep all the headings in the same order.
If any information is missing, write [TO FILL]. Do not make anything up.

FORMAT:
[paste your blank format]

DETAILS:
[type your details]

This helps in three ways:

  1. The answer follows your layout, so you do less editing.
  2. The AI does not have to guess the structure.
  3. [TO FILL] shows you exactly what is missing, instead of hiding made-up details.
Traffic Light Rule: paste only the blank format and details that are not personal. Never paste students' or staff members' personal data.

5. Phrases that stop AI from making things up

Add one or more of these when correct facts matter:

  • "Use only the information I have given."
  • "If you are not sure, say so. Do not guess."
  • "Do not make up any names, dates or numbers."
  • "Mark anything you are not sure about with [CHECK]."

These phrases do not make the AI perfect. But they reduce made-up details and make your checking faster.

Let the AI ask you questions

For a bigger job, try this:

I need to prepare [the job]. Before you write anything, ask me all the questions you need answered.

The AI lists what it needs to know. You answer. The final result is much better.

6. Ready-to-use prompts (copy, paste, fill the blanks)

Prompt 2.1: The 4-part request
You are [who]. Details: [who will read it, why, dates, places, limits]. Job: [what you want written]. Look: [length, tone, layout]. Use only the information I have given. Write [TO FILL] for anything missing.
Prompt 2.2: Make it clearer
Rewrite the text below for [who will read it]. Make it [clear / polite / formal], under [number] words. Keep all facts, dates and names the same. TEXT: [paste]
Prompt 2.3: Fill my college's format
Below is our [type of document] format. Fill it in using the details given. Keep all headings in the same order. Write [TO FILL] for anything missing. Do not make anything up. FORMAT: [paste blank format] DETAILS: [type details]
Prompt 2.4: Ask me first
I need to prepare [the job] for [who will read it]. Before you write, ask me all the questions you need answered, in one list.

See the difference

Situation: The office at Tarangini Institute of Technology (a made-up college) needs a circular for an IoT workshop.

Weak request

Write a circular about an IoT workshop.

Typical answer (shortened):

CIRCULAR
All students are informed that a workshop on Internet of Things will be held on [Date] at [Venue]. Interested students may register. For details contact [Contact Person].

Too general, full of blanks, and not in the college's style. Sometimes the AI fills the blanks with made-up dates and places, which is worse.

Clear request

You are an office assistant at an engineering college.
Details: The ECE department of Tarangini Institute of Technology is holding a 2-day hands-on workshop, "IoT with ESP32", for III B.Tech students on 14–15 November, 9:30 am to 4:30 pm, in Seminar Hall 2. Free. Only 60 seats. Register at the ECE office by 10 November. Students must bring their own laptops.
Job: Write a circular to announce this workshop to students.
Look: Formal, under 150 words. Reference number and date lines as [TO FILL], a subject line, the main details as a short list, and a signature line for the Head of Department.
Use only the information given. Do not make up any details.

Typical answer (shortened):

Ref: [TO FILL]   Date: [TO FILL]
Subject: Two-day hands-on workshop on "IoT with ESP32" for III B.Tech students

The Department of ECE is pleased to announce a two-day hands-on workshop on IoT with ESP32.

- Dates: 14–15 November
- Time: 9:30 am – 4:30 pm
- Venue: Seminar Hall 2
- Who can join: III B.Tech students (60 seats only)
- Registration: Free, at the ECE office, by 10 November
- Note: Please bring your own laptop.

Interested students are requested to register early.

Head of the Department, ECE

Almost ready to send. Check the facts, fill in the reference number and date, and it is done.

(Every AI tool writes a little differently, and the answer changes each time. Your answer will not match this word for word. That is normal.)

Try it (15 minutes)

  1. Pick a real job from your work this week: a notice, an email, a lab instruction or a report paragraph.
  2. Write a short, one-line request and try it. Save the answer.
  3. Download the Prompt Builder Worksheet. Fill in the 4 parts for the same job.
  4. Try the clear request. Compare the two answers.
  5. Ask for two changes, like "shorter" or "more formal."
  6. Extra: Try Prompt 2.3 with a blank format from your own office or lab (no personal data).

Save your best prompt. You will add it to your prompt collection in Module 4.

Remember these points

  • The AI knows only what you tell it. Missing details cause most poor answers.
  • A clear request has 4 parts: Who, Details, Job, Look.
  • Show an example of the style you want, and ask for changes to the first draft.
  • Paste your college's format and ask the AI to fill it, with [TO FILL] for missing information.
  • Phrases like "use only the information given" reduce made-up details.
